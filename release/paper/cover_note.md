## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

A long literature asks what British by-elections reveal about voters; this paper asks what they do to Parliament. Linking every UK by-election since 1909 in the Wikipedia lists to monthly Hansard counts of Commons contributions that mention the town named in the seat, and comparing 387 by-election towns with matched towns in a stacked event study specified in a written plan before the analysis, it finds that mentions rise during the vacancy, peak around polling day at about 4.0 times the matched level, and stay about 1.9 times higher a year later and 1.6 times higher in the second year, before fading. A change of hands enlarges the first jump but not the lasting attention; government- and opposition-held seats do not differ; and there is no detectable rise before 1945. Placebo dates, neighbouring towns and the seat-name phrase show no comparable change. The design is observational, and data and code are openly released.
